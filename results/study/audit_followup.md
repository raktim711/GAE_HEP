## 1. How much the fit-quality curve explains

Curve: quadratic in the background validation loss, fitted to the E1 + E2 runs (seven encoders, 1, 2, 3 and 5 epochs). `R² curve`: share of the AUC variance between these runs that the curve explains; `R² configuration`: share explained by knowing only each run's configuration (encoder × training length, the mean of its seeds); the leave-one-out versions predict each run by the curve refitted without it and by the mean of the other seeds of its configuration (the fair comparison: the configuration means are 28 parameters); `configuration on residual`: share of the curve's residual variance that the configuration still explains. Within-configuration test: loss and AUC with every configuration's mean removed (seeds change the fit at fixed architecture and training length). Out of sample: the E4 ablation runs, which the curve was not fitted to; R² < 0 means the curve predicts them worse than their mean.

## 6. Look-elsewhere calibration of the BB1 bump hunt

The BB1 hunt (E3) trains on BB1's own events and tests the known window (3.6–4.0 TeV) with cuts keeping 10 % and 2 % of the sideband events. Here the same procedure is run without signal: R&D-trained runs (study_efn_s1, study_efn_s2, study_efn_s3) scan their own training sample, the whole R&D background (1 000 000 events), over 14 windows of 400 GeV (lower edges 3.0–4.3 TeV) × the same cuts. 3000 toy experiments from smooth fits of the selected spectra give the distribution of the largest local Z of the scan (windows × {10 %, 2 %}). The toys and the `converged` columns minimise the likelihood fully (L-BFGS-B then BFGS, started at the analysis optimum); the analysis fit of `bumphunt.search` (Nelder–Mead then Powell) can stop short of the minimum, and the last columns give how much that moves the local Z of these spectra (42 tests per run).
